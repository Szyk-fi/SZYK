Why ROVE

The best of what you own, and what comes next.

ROVE doesn’t replace your gear. It connects to it. Legacy equipment is why we’re here, and we’re moving forward while embracing the past.

  1. 01

    Talk to your modular.

    Send CV and gates out to the rack you’ve already built, and bring voltages back in. ROVE’s sequencers, generators and hand sensors become new sources of movement for your modular synth.

  2. 02

    Direct the synths you love.

    With MIDI in and out, ROVE can stand in front of the instruments you already play. Use it as a possibility machine: generate patterns, morph parameters, and let your classic gear do what it does best.

  3. 03

    One engine, every idea.

    Inside, every app shares one real-time engine and one modulation bus, so anything can feed anything. Instruments, effects and sequencers behave like parts of a single instrument.

  4. 04

    Hands-on, wherever you play.

    A pad deck, D-pad, stick and two hand sensors keep you making music, not managing menus, whether you’re at the rack, on the couch or on the road.

// the controls arrive once per frame; audio runs on its own thread
fn tick(&mut self, input: &Input) {
    self.held.store(input.grid[0], Ordering::Relaxed);
    if input.knob1 != 0 {
        let next = (self.freq.get() * 1.05f32.powf(input.knob1 as f32)).clamp(MIN_FREQ, MAX_FREQ);
        self.freq.set(next);
    }
}

// the screen is yours: 640 x 360
fn draw(&mut self, fb: &mut FrameBuffer) {
    Text::new("Template", Point::new(20, 30), title).draw(fb).ok();
    Text::new(&format!("Frequency {:.0} Hz", self.freq.get()), Point::new(20, 60), dim).draw(fb).ok();
}

// the audio callback: never blocks, never allocates
fn process(&mut self, buffer: &mut [f32], channels: usize, sample_rate: f32) {
    for frame in buffer.chunks_mut(channels) {
        let sample = (self.phase * TAU).sin() * self.amp * volume;
        self.phase = (self.phase + freq / sample_rate).fract();
        for out in frame.iter_mut() { *out = sample; }
    }
}

Specifications

What’s in the box.

Display800 × 480 landscape multitouch screen (proposed). App raster artwork keeps its native proportions.
ProcessorSTM32N6, with the neural processing unit used by the AI apps.
Play surface4×4 pad deck, D-pad, analog stick, L1 / R1, touchscreen action buttons, two depth sensors for hand control, and a small camera above the pad deck.
ExpansionFour card slots. Cards can add MIDI in/out, CV in/out, sensors, audio in/out and more, and you choose your four.
Audio engineOne real-time engine and one modulation bus shared by every app, with a mixer channel per app.
SoftwareInstruments, effects and sequencers today, plus your own. Open source.

Design targets. ROVE is in development and final specifications may change before production.

Questions

Good questions.

Is ROVE a real device yet?

ROVE is the hardware we’re crowdfunding. The software already runs today in a full desktop simulator, built so the architecture could be proven before any hardware exists. The device is designed around an STM32N6 processor.

Is the demo above the real thing?

It’s the real interface, with different sound engines. The screens and controls are ported from the real apps, but the audio here is a lightweight Web Audio version made for this page. On ROVE, every app runs on the native engine.

Do all of these apps ship?

The 20 apps in the preview are a selection. What’s installed at launch will be listed on the campaign page.

Can I really make my own app?

Yes. You can start from a template, or start from the ground up. Some apps just need presets or scripts added to them. And if your coding skills aren’t developed enough yet, an AI system like Claude or ChatGPT is an easy way to write one with you. Apps you make can be shared with the community.

Is it really open source?

Yes. The whole system is open source, so you have access to all of the code, and you can even rewrite the entire ecosystem if you want to. ROVE’s own code is MIT licensed; bundled third-party components keep their own licences.

Will there be a community for apps?

Yes. There will be a community where people can post the apps they create, and rate and review the apps others have made.

Can ROVE work with my modular and my other synths?

That’s a big part of the idea. With the right cards, ROVE can send and receive CV and MIDI, so it can sit in front of your modular system and your existing synths as a possibility machine.

How do audio, MIDI and CV get in and out?

Through expansion cards. ROVE has four card slots, and cards can include extra MIDI in/out, CV in/out, sensors, audio in/out and more. You choose your four, so the device fits the way you play.

What does it cost, and when does it ship?

Pricing, perk tiers and delivery estimates are on the Indiegogo campaign page, which is always the source of truth.
